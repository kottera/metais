# Revised Section 508 criteria

What each criterion of chapters 3 to 6 of the Revised Section 508
Standards (36 CFR part 1194, appendix C) asks, one section per criterion,
in the standards' order, with the criterion's number and its heading.
These are the values the `criterion` column of the **section508** input
takes.

Chapter 3 holds the functional performance criteria, chapter 4 hardware,
chapter 5 software and chapter 6 support documentation and services.
The provisions that apply WCAG to electronic content and to software
(E205.4 and E207.2) are answered by the WCAG 2.2 table, so this list holds
what that table does not. Criteria whose only role is to group the ones beneath them, and
412.5, which the standards reserve, are not listed.

Each summary is the METAIS project's own wording of what the criterion
asks; the standards' text governs, at
<https://www.access-board.gov/ict/>. `sources.md` records this source and
its terms.

## 302.1 Without Vision

At least one mode of operation works without any vision.

## 302.2 With Limited Vision

At least one mode of operation lets people with limited vision enlarge, contrast or otherwise use what is shown.

## 302.3 Without Perception of Color

At least one visual mode of operation works without telling colours apart.

## 302.4 Without Hearing

At least one mode of operation works without hearing, where the product produces sound.

## 302.5 With Limited Hearing

At least one mode of operation offers better audio for people with limited hearing, where the product produces sound.

## 302.6 Without Speech

At least one mode of operation works without the user speaking, where the product takes speech input.

## 302.7 With Limited Manipulation

At least one mode of operation works without fine motor control or simultaneous actions.

## 302.8 With Limited Reach and Strength

At least one mode of operation works with limited reach and limited strength.

## 302.9 With Limited Language, Cognitive, and Learning Abilities

The product offers features that make it simpler and easier to use for people with limited language, cognitive or learning abilities.

## 402.1 Closed Functionality: General

Hardware or software the user cannot add assistive technology to meets the closed-functionality provisions.

## 402.2 Speech-Output Enabled

Closed products with a display offer speech output.

## 402.2.1 Information Displayed On-Screen

Speech output covers what the screen shows, with stated exceptions.

## 402.2.2 Transactional Outputs

Speech output covers receipts, tickets and other transactional outputs.

## 402.2.3 Speech Delivery Type and Coordination

Speech is delivered through a private channel and kept in step with the display.

## 402.2.4 User Control

Users can pause, restart and interrupt the speech output.

## 402.2.5 Braille Instructions

Braille instructions say how to start the speech output.

## 402.3 Volume

Closed products that give speech output let the user control its volume.

## 402.3.1 Private Listening

Private listening has a volume control with a stated range and gain.

## 402.3.2 Non-private Listening

Speaker output has a volume control with a stated range.

## 402.4 Characters on Display Screens

Text on a closed product's screen is in a sans serif font of a stated minimum height.

## 402.5 Characters on Variable Message Signs

Characters on variable message signs meet the stated visual requirements.

## 403.1 Biometrics

Where biometrics identify a user, another method that does not rely on the same characteristic is offered.

## 404.1 Preservation of Information Provided for Accessibility

Information provided for accessibility is not removed when content is transmitted or converted.

## 405.1 Privacy

Accessibility features give the same privacy as other features.

## 406.1 Standard Connections

Data connections to devices users bring meet an industry standard.

## 407.2 Contrast

Keys and controls stand out visually from their surroundings.

## 407.3 Input Controls

Input controls can be found and operated by touch.

## 407.3.1 Tactilely Discernible

Input controls can be told apart by touch without being activated.

## 407.3.2 Alphabetic Keys

Alphabetic keys are laid out in a conventional arrangement.

## 407.3.3 Numeric Keys

Numeric keys follow a conventional layout and the 5 key is tactilely marked.

## 407.4 Key Repeat

Key repeat, where offered, can be slowed to a stated delay.

## 407.5 Timed Response

Users are alerted when a timed response is needed and given time to ask for more.

## 407.6 Operation

Operable parts work with one hand, without tight grasping or twisting, within a stated force.

## 407.7 Tickets, Fare Cards, and Keycards

Tickets, fare cards and keycards have an orientation that can be found by touch.

## 407.8 Reach Height and Depth

Operable parts of stationary products are within a reach that is stated in the provisions below.

## 407.8.1 Vertical Reference Plane

Reach is measured from a stated vertical reference plane.

## 407.8.1.1 Vertical Plane for Side Reach

Side reach is measured from a stated plane beside the product.

## 407.8.1.2 Vertical Plane for Forward Reach

Forward reach is measured from a stated plane in front of the product.

## 407.8.2 Side Reach

Operable parts reachable from the side are within stated heights.

## 407.8.2.1 Unobstructed Side Reach

Unobstructed side reach is within stated heights.

## 407.8.2.2 Obstructed Side Reach

Side reach over an obstruction is within stated heights and depths.

## 407.8.3 Forward Reach

Operable parts reachable from the front are within stated heights.

## 407.8.3.1 Unobstructed Forward Reach

Unobstructed forward reach is within stated heights.

## 407.8.3.2 Obstructed Forward Reach

Forward reach over an obstruction is within stated heights and depths.

## 407.8.3.2.1 Operable Part Height for ICT with Obstructed Forward Reach

Operable parts reached over an obstruction are at a stated height.

## 407.8.3.2.2 Knee and Toe Space under ICT with Obstructed Forward Reach

Knee and toe space under the obstruction meets stated dimensions.

## 408.2 Visibility

A display screen at a stationary product is visible from a point at a stated height.

## 408.3 Flashing

The screen does not flash more than three times in any one second, or stays below the flash thresholds.

## 409.1 Status Indicators

Locking and toggle controls show their status visually and by touch or sound.

## 410.1 Color Coding

Colour is not the only means of conveying information or prompting action.

## 411.1 Audible Signals

An audible signal used to prompt or convey information also has a visual equivalent.

## 412.2 Volume Gain

Voice communication products offer volume gain.

## 412.2.1 Volume Gain for Wireline Telephones

Wireline telephones offer volume gain to the stated standard.

## 412.2.2 Volume Gain for Non-Wireline ICT

Other voice products offer a stated minimum gain.

## 412.3 Interference Reduction and Magnetic Coupling

Handsets reduce interference with hearing technologies and couple magnetically to them.

## 412.3.1 Wireless Handsets

Wireless handsets meet the stated hearing-aid compatibility standard.

## 412.3.2 Wireline Handsets

Wireline handsets meet the stated hearing-aid compatibility standard.

## 412.4 Digital Encoding of Speech

Speech is encoded to the stated standard where voice is carried over IP.

## 412.6 Caller ID

Caller identification and similar telecommunication functions are available visually and audibly.

## 412.7 Video Communication

Video communication supports the resolution, frame rate and synchronization sign language needs.

## 412.8 Legacy TTY Support

Voice products support legacy TTY use where stated.

## 412.8.1 TTY Connectability

A TTY can be connected directly.

## 412.8.2 Voice and Hearing Carry Over

Voice carry over and hearing carry over are supported.

## 412.8.3 Signal Compatibility

TTY signals are compatible with the stated standard.

## 412.8.4 Voice Mail and Other Messaging Systems

Voice mail, auto-attendant and interactive voice response systems work with TTYs.

## 413.1.1 Decoding and Display of Closed Captions

Players and displays decode and show closed captions.

## 413.1.2 Pass-Through of Closed Caption Data

Products that transmit or convert video keep the closed caption data.

## 414.1.1 Digital Television Tuners

Digital television tuners can play audio description.

## 414.1.2 Other ICT

Other products that play video can play audio description.

## 415.1.1 Caption Controls

Caption controls are at the same menu level as the volume and program controls.

## 415.1.2 Audio Description Controls

Audio description controls are at the same menu level as the volume and program controls.

## 502.2.1 User Control of Accessibility Features

Platform software lets users control its accessibility features.

## 502.2.2 No Disruption of Accessibility Features

Software does not disrupt the platform's accessibility features.

## 502.3 Accessibility Services

Platform software and its development tools provide services that let assistive technology work with applications.

## 502.3.1 Object Information

The role, state, properties, boundary, name and description of objects are determinable by software.

## 502.3.2 Modification of Object Information

States and properties the user can set can also be set by software.

## 502.3.3 Row, Column, and Headers

The row and column of a data table's cell, and its headers, are determinable by software.

## 502.3.4 Values

Current values, and any set of allowed values, are determinable by software.

## 502.3.5 Modification of Values

Values the user can set can also be set by software.

## 502.3.6 Label Relationships

Which component labels or is labelled by another is determinable by software.

## 502.3.7 Hierarchical Relationships

The parent and child relationships of components are determinable by software.

## 502.3.8 Text

Text content, its attributes and the text cursor's boundary are determinable by software.

## 502.3.9 Modification of Text

Text the user can change can also be changed by software.

## 502.3.10 List of Actions

The actions an object offers are determinable by software.

## 502.3.11 Actions on Objects

The actions an object offers can be carried out by software.

## 502.3.12 Focus Cursor

The focus cursor's position and the focused object are determinable by software.

## 502.3.13 Modification of Focus Cursor

The focus cursor can be moved by software.

## 502.3.14 Event Notification

Software is notified of events relevant to the user, such as changes of focus, state and content.

## 502.4 Platform Accessibility Features

Platforms provide stated accessibility features, such as sticky keys and adjustable key repeat.

## 503.2 User Preferences

Applications follow the colour, contrast, font, size and focus settings the user chose on the platform.

## 503.3 Alternative User Interfaces

An alternative interface that works as assistive technology uses the platform's accessibility services.

## 503.4 User Controls for Captions and Audio Description

Software that plays media gives users control of captions and audio description.

## 503.4.1 Caption Controls

Caption controls are at the same menu level as the volume and program controls.

## 503.4.2 Audio Description Controls

Audio description controls are at the same menu level as the volume and program controls.

## 504.2 Content Creation or Editing

Authoring tools offer a way to create accessible content.

## 504.2.1 Preservation of Information Provided for Accessibility in Format Conversion

Authoring tools keep accessibility information when they convert content between formats that support it.

## 504.2.2 PDF Export

Authoring tools that export PDF can export it to the stated accessible PDF standard.

## 504.3 Prompts

Authoring tools prompt authors to create accessible content.

## 504.4 Templates

Authoring tools that offer templates offer accessible ones for a range of uses.

## 602.2 Accessibility and Compatibility Features

Documentation lists and explains the product's accessibility and compatibility features.

## 602.3 Electronic Support Documentation

Electronic documentation meets the WCAG criteria at Level A and AA.

## 602.4 Alternate Formats for Non-Electronic Support Documentation

Paper documentation is available in alternate formats on request.

## 603.2 Information on Accessibility and Compatibility Features

Support services give information on the product's accessibility and compatibility features.

## 603.3 Accommodation of Communication Needs

Support services accommodate the communication needs of people with disabilities.
