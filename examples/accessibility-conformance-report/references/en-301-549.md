# EN 301 549 clauses

What each clause of EN 301 549 V4.1.1 (2026-09), *Accessibility
requirements for ICT products and services*, that a report answers
outside the WCAG table asks, one section per clause, in the standard's
order. These are the values the `criterion` column of the **en301549**
input takes.

The clause numbers are the standard's, used as identifiers. Every name
and summary here is the METAIS project's own description of the clause,
in its own words; none is ETSI's heading or text. The standard itself
governs, and is published by ETSI at
<https://www.etsi.org/deliver/etsi_en/301500_301599/301549/04.01.01_60/en_301549v040101p.pdf>.
`sources.md` records this source and its terms.

Clauses 9 (web), 10 (non-web documents) and 11.1 to 11.4 (software)
apply the WCAG 2.2 criteria, and the WCAG 2.2 table answers them.
Clauses that only group the ones beneath them, that are informative or
void, or that only describe scenarios (6.0) or point to other clauses
(8.1) are not listed. A clause the standard marks as a recommendation
says so in its summary.

## 4.2.1 Operating the product without sight

Some mode of operation can be used by a person who cannot see.

## 4.2.2 Operating the product with low vision

Some mode of operation can be used by a person with limited vision, such as through magnification, contrast or a different layout.

## 4.2.3 Operating the product without telling colours apart

Some visual mode of operation does not require the user to tell colours apart.

## 4.2.4 Operating the product without sound

Where the product makes sound, some mode of operation can be used by a person who cannot hear.

## 4.2.5 Operating the product with reduced hearing

Where the product makes sound, some mode of operation gives better audio for a person with limited hearing.

## 4.2.6 Operating the product without speaking

Where the product takes spoken input, some mode of operation does not require the user to speak.

## 4.2.7 Operating the product with limited dexterity or strength

Some mode of operation can be used without fine motor control, strength or several simultaneous actions.

## 4.2.8 Operating the product from a limited reach

The operable parts of a product that stands in place can be reached by everyone it is meant for.

## 4.2.9 Keeping flashing below seizure risk

Where the product shows visual content, some mode of operation keeps flashing low enough not to trigger seizures.

## 4.2.10 Operating the product with cognitive or learning disabilities, or little command of its language

The product offers features that make it simpler and easier to use for people with limited cognition, language or learning.

## 4.2.11 The same privacy for users of accessibility features

A person using an accessibility feature keeps the same privacy as anyone else.

## 5.1.2.1 Closed products meet the closed-product clauses

Where a user cannot attach assistive technology, the product meets the closed-functionality clauses itself.

## 5.1.2.2 Closed products need no added assistive technology

Nothing of a closed product requires the user to attach or install assistive technology.

## 5.1.3.1 Closed products: what is shown is also spoken

Where a closed product shows information that is needed to use it, it also gives that information as sound.

## 5.1.3.2 Closed products: how speech reaches the user

Speech output can be heard through the product's own speaker or a headset, as the user chooses.

## 5.1.3.3 Closed products: speech matches the screen

Spoken output corresponds to what is shown and where it is shown.

## 5.1.3.4 Closed products: pausing and repeating speech

Users can pause and repeat the speech output.

## 5.1.3.5 Closed products: speech gives way to the user

Speech output stops when the user acts, unless it is essential.

## 5.1.3.6 Closed products: speaking images and other non-text content

Non-text content shown to the user is also described in speech.

## 5.1.3.7 Closed products: speaking what video shows

Information that pre-recorded video shows is also given in speech.

## 5.1.3.8 Closed products: hidden characters stay hidden

Masked characters are not spoken unless the user chooses, and are spoken as masked.

## 5.1.3.9 Closed products: personal data spoken privately

Personal data given in speech is given privately.

## 5.1.3.10 Closed products: other sounds do not drown out speech

Other sound the product makes does not drown out its speech output.

## 5.1.3.11 Closed products: volume for private listening

Private listening has a volume control.

## 5.1.3.12 Closed products: loudspeaker volume

Speaker output has a volume control with sufficient range.

## 5.1.3.13 Closed products: volume back to normal between users

A product shared by many users resets its volume between users.

## 5.1.3.14 Closed products: speech in the content's language

Speech is in the same language as the content shown.

## 5.1.3.15 Closed products: errors announced in speech

Errors shown to the user are also identified in speech.

## 5.1.3.16 Closed products: receipts and tickets in speech

Receipts, tickets and other transactional outputs are also available in speech.

## 5.1.4 Closed products: text large enough without enlargement

Where a closed product cannot enlarge text, its text is large enough as shown.

## 5.1.5 Closed products: sound information also shown

Information a closed product gives by sound is also given visually.

## 5.1.6.1 Closed products: use without a keyboard

A closed product can be operated without a keyboard or its equivalent.

## 5.1.6.2 Closed products: focus never trapped

Where a closed product moves input focus, the focus is never trapped.

## 5.1.7 Closed products: another way than speaking

A closed product that takes speech offers another way to give the same input.

## 5.1.8 Closed products: the purpose of personal fields spoken

Where a closed product asks for information about the user, some mode of operation tells the user in sound what each such field is for.

## 5.2 Turning on accessibility features without them

An accessibility feature can be turned on without the help of that feature.

## 5.3 Identifying users by the body

Where a biological characteristic identifies a user, another way of identifying is offered.

## 5.4 Accessibility information survives conversion

Information provided for accessibility survives a conversion of the content.

## 5.5 Controls that can be found and used by touch

Everything operated by hand can also be operated without sight, using controls that can be told apart by touch.

## 5.6.1 Locking and toggle controls: state by touch or sound

The state of a locking or toggle control can be told by touch or sound.

## 5.6.2 Locking and toggle controls: state by sight

The state of a locking or toggle control can be told by sight.

## 5.7 Repeating keys adjustable

Where keys repeat, the delay before repeating and the rate can be adjusted.

## 5.8 Accepting a doubled keystroke

Where a key accepts a double strike, the interval in which it does can be adjusted.

## 5.9 No need to act in two places at once

No mode of operation requires the user to act in more than one place at once.

## 5.10.1 Authoring tools: formats that support accessibility

Authoring tools support the accessibility features of the formats they produce.

## 5.10.2 Authoring tools: making accessible content

Authoring tools let authors create content that meets the clauses for web content, documents and software.

## 5.10.3 Authoring tools: accessibility kept through transformation

Authoring tools keep accessibility information when they transform content.

## 5.10.4 Authoring tools: help fixing problems

Authoring tools that check accessibility suggest how to fix what they find.

## 5.10.5 Authoring tools: accessible starting points

Authoring tools that offer templates offer some that produce accessible content.

## 6.1.1 Voice calls: interoperability documented

Where the product carries voice calls to other products, its documentation names the specifications that let its voice work with theirs.

## 6.1.2 Voice calls: frequency range

Voice is captured, carried and played over a wide enough frequency range.

## 6.1.3 Voice calls: frequency range for attached headsets

A recommendation: voice played through an attached listening device covers a wide enough frequency range.

## 6.1.4 Voice calls: subtitles in step

A recommendation: subtitles of a call follow the speech within a short delay.

## 6.2.1.1 Real-time text: offered beside voice

Where the product offers voice calls, it also lets users communicate in real-time text.

## 6.2.1.2 Real-time text: voice and text together

Where voice and real-time text are both offered, they can be used at the same time.

## 6.2.1.3 Real-time text: one action for every medium

One user action starts, answers, changes or ends a call with all its media, real-time text included.

## 6.2.2.1 Real-time text: telling sent from received

Text sent and text received are displayed so that they can be told apart.

## 6.2.2.2 Real-time text: showing who is communicating

Where speakers are indicated, everyone communicating is indicated, those typing included.

## 6.2.2.3 Real-time text: showing when there is sound

An indicator shows when there is audio activity during a real-time text call.

## 6.2.2.4 Real-time text: blocks in the order written

Text from each party is shown in blocks, ordered by when each block was finished.

## 6.2.2.5 Real-time text: reading back the conversation

The whole text of the call can be read back during it, and afterwards when it is recorded.

## 6.2.3 Real-time text: touch-tones kept apart from text

Touch-tone signals can be sent by a means distinct from typing the same characters in real-time text.

## 6.2.4 Real-time text: sent without delay

Each character is sent within a short stated delay after it is typed.

## 6.2.5 Real-time text: typing and deleting

Users can type new text and delete what they typed, back from the end, during the call.

## 6.2.6 Real-time text: keeping up with several parties

The product can receive text from several parties at once at a stated total rate.

## 6.2.7 Real-time text: the characters it handles

The product handles at least the characters the clause names, those of its markets' languages included.

## 6.2.8 Real-time text: every way of typing

Real-time text can be entered in every way the product offers for entering text.

## 6.2.9 Real-time text: always available in a call

Real-time text can be requested, accepted and used in a call whatever the settings.

## 6.2.10 Real-time text: interoperability documented

Where the product connects to other products for calls, its documentation names the specifications that let its real-time text work with theirs.

## 6.3 Caller identification in text

Caller identification and similar information is also available as text that assistive technology can read.

## 6.4 Voice services: an alternative to voice

Services based on voice offer an alternative that does not rely on hearing or speech.

## 6.5.2 Video calls: picture resolution

Video is sent at a sufficient resolution.

## 6.5.3 Video calls: frames per second

Video is sent at a sufficient frame rate.

## 6.5.4 Video calls: sound and picture in step

Sound and picture stay within a stated time of one another.

## 6.5.5 Video calls: showing when there is sound

An indicator shows when there is audio activity during a video call.

## 6.5.6 Video calls: showing who is communicating

Where speakers are indicated, everyone communicating is indicated, those signing included.

## 6.6 Video services: an alternative to video

A recommendation: services based on video offer an alternative that does not rely on sight or hearing alone.

## 6.7.1 Voice, video and text in one call

Where the product offers voice and video calls, it also offers real-time text in them, so all three can be used together.

## 6.7.2 Voice, video and text: interoperability documented

Where the product connects to other products for video calls, its documentation names the specifications that let its video work with theirs.

## 7.1.1 Subtitles: shown when present

Products that play video can show its subtitles.

## 7.1.2 Subtitles: in step with the sound

Subtitles are shown in step with the sound they convey.

## 7.1.3 Subtitles: not lost on the way

Products that convert or pass on video keep its subtitles.

## 7.1.4 Subtitles: appearance adjustable

Users can adjust how subtitles look.

## 7.1.5 Subtitles read aloud

Where video carries subtitles, they can be spoken.

## 7.2.1 Audio description: played when present

Products that play video can play its audio description.

## 7.2.2 Audio description: in step with the picture

Audio description is played in step with the video.

## 7.2.3 Audio description: not lost on the way

Products that convert or pass on video keep its audio description.

## 7.3 Controls for subtitles and audio description

Subtitles and audio description can be turned on at the same level of interaction as the main media controls.

## 8.2.1.1 Hardware speech: amplification

Voice output can be made loud enough, over a stated range of gain.

## 8.2.1.2 Hardware speech: raising the volume in steps

The volume can be raised in small steps.

## 8.2.2.1 Hearing-aid coupling: fixed-line products

Fixed-line devices and handsets couple magnetically with hearing technologies.

## 8.2.2.2 Hearing-aid coupling: wireless products

Wireless devices couple magnetically with hearing technologies.

## 8.3.2 Stationary products: how low a screen may sit

No part of a display screen sits below a stated height above the floor.

## 8.3.3.1 Stationary products: how high information may sit

Information needed to operate the product is placed no higher than a stated height.

## 8.3.3.2 Stationary products: how high information may sit, where design limits it

Where the design cannot meet the lower height, information needed to operate the product is placed no higher than a greater stated height.

## 8.3.5.1 Stationary products: height of controls

At least one control of each kind lies within a stated band of heights.

## 8.3.5.2 Stationary products: height of controls, where design limits it

Where the design cannot meet that band, controls that cannot be duplicated lie within a wider stated band.

## 8.3.6 Stationary products: width of the control area

At least one control of each kind lies within an area of a stated width.

## 8.3.7.1 Stationary products: depth of the control area

At least one control of each kind lies within a stated distance of the nearest edge.

## 8.3.7.2 Stationary products: depth of the control area, where design limits it

Where the design cannot meet that distance, controls lie within greater stated distances for forward and side reach.

## 8.3.9.2 Stationary products: reaching forward over an obstruction

An obstruction in front of the controls keeps within stated heights and depths.

## 8.3.9.3 Stationary products: reaching forward over a deep obstruction

Where the design needs a deeper obstruction, it keeps within other stated heights and depths.

## 8.3.10.2 Stationary products: reaching sideways over an obstruction

An obstruction beside the controls keeps within stated heights and depths.

## 8.3.10.3 Stationary products: reaching sideways over a deep obstruction

Where the design needs a deeper obstruction, it keeps within other stated heights and depths.

## 8.3.11.1 Stationary products: room for knees and toes

The space beneath the product leaves clearance for knees and toes of stated dimensions.

## 8.3.11.2 Stationary products: room for knees and toes, where design limits it

Where the design restricts that space, the clearance still meets other stated dimensions.

## 8.3.14 Stationary products: guidance for installers

Instructions say how to install the product so that it meets the clauses on physical access.

## 8.4.1 Number keys laid out as usual

Numeric keys are laid out conventionally and the 5 key can be found by touch.

## 8.4.2.1 Mechanical controls: no grip or twist needed

Mechanical parts can be operated with one hand and without tight grasping, pinching or twisting.

## 8.4.2.2 Mechanical controls: light enough to press

Mechanical parts can be operated with no more than a stated force.

## 8.4.3 Cards and tickets that can be oriented by touch

Keys, tickets and cards can be oriented by touch.

## 8.5 Finding the speech mode by touch

A product that offers speech output shows by touch how to start it.

## 8.6 Connections that follow an industry standard

Where the product has connections for devices, at least one follows a non-proprietary industry standard.

## 8.7 Hardware: colour never the only cue

Colour on the hardware is not the only means of conveying information or prompting action.

## 8.8 Hardware: markings that stand out

Printed characters and symbols, and unlabelled controls that cannot be found by touch, stand out from their background.

## 11.5.2.1 Platforms: accessibility services for assistive technology

Platform software offers documented accessibility services that cover the user interface concepts of the clauses below.

## 11.5.2.3 Software: using the platform's services

A recommendation: software uses the platform's documented accessibility services.

## 11.5.2.4 Assistive technology: using the platform's services

Assistive technology uses the platform's documented accessibility services.

## 11.5.2.5 Software exposes what each element is

The role, states, boundary, name and description of user interface elements are determinable by software.

## 11.5.2.6 Software exposes table cells and headers

The row and column of a table cell, and its headers, are determinable by software.

## 11.5.2.7 Software exposes current values

The current value of an element, and any allowed range or set, are determinable by software.

## 11.5.2.8 Software exposes which element labels which

Which element labels or is labelled by another is determinable by software.

## 11.5.2.9 Software exposes how elements nest

The parent and child relationships of elements are determinable by software.

## 11.5.2.10 Software exposes its text

Text content, its attributes and its boundary on screen are determinable by software.

## 11.5.2.11 Software exposes the actions an element offers

The actions an element offers are determinable by software.

## 11.5.2.12 Software lets assistive technology carry out actions

The actions an element offers can be carried out by software.

## 11.5.2.13 Software exposes focus and selection

Focus and selection, and how they change, are determinable by software.

## 11.5.2.14 Software lets assistive technology move focus and selection

Focus and selection can be changed by software, where the user can change them.

## 11.5.2.15 Software announces changes

Assistive technology is notified of changes to the information above.

## 11.5.2.16 Software lets assistive technology change states

States and properties the user can change can also be changed by software.

## 11.5.2.17 Software lets assistive technology change values and text

Values and text the user can change can also be changed by software.

## 11.6.1 Software: users control its accessibility features

Software that offers accessibility features lets the user control them.

## 11.6.2 Software: the platform's accessibility features left working

Software does not disrupt the platform's accessibility features.

## 11.7 Software follows the user's platform settings

Software follows the user's platform settings for colour, contrast, font, size and focus.

## 12.1 Digital information about the product is accessible

Information about the product given in digital form meets the clauses for web content, documents or software, as its form requires.

## 12.2 Digital information on a service is accessible

Information about a service, support services included, given in digital form meets the clauses for web content, documents or software, as its form requires.

## 12.3 Documentation explains the accessibility features

The product's documentation says, directly or by a link, what its accessibility features are, how to use them and how to get help with them.

## 12.4 Programme guides say which access services there are

An electronic programme guide tells the viewer which programmes come with subtitles, audio description, spoken subtitles or signing.

## 12.5 Showing which access services a programme has

A product that shows a programme guide can show which access services each programme has.

## 13.1.2.2 Relay services: users keep their own number

Calls placed through a relay service use the address each user's own communication system gave them.

## 13.1.2.3 Relay services: the caller is still identified

The caller's identification is passed on when a call goes through a relay service.

## 13.1.2.4 Relay services: choosing relay when calling

A user placing a call can choose, at that moment, whether a relay service joins it.

## 13.1.2.5 Relay services: choosing relay when answering

A user receiving a call can choose, at that moment, whether a relay service joins it.

## 13.1.2.6 Relay services: relay in an emergency call

A user can place an emergency call with a relay service joining it.

## 13.1.3.1 Relay services: converting between modes in a voice call

A relay service lets its users take part in spoken calls by converting between modes of communication.

## 13.1.3.2 Relay services: other media passed through

Media the relay does not convert still reaches both parties.

## 13.1.3.3 Relay services: taking part in conferences

A relay service lets its users take part in conference calls.

## 13.1.3.4 Relay services: called in by emergency services

A relay service accepts being called into a call by an emergency service centre.
