# EN 301 549 clauses

What each clause of EN 301 549 V3.2.1 (2021-03), *Accessibility
requirements for ICT products and services*, that a report answers
outside the WCAG table asks, one section per clause, in the standard's
order, named by its number. These are the values the `criterion` column
of the **en301549** input takes.

Every name and summary here is the METAIS project's own description of
the clause, in its own words; none is ETSI's heading or text. The
standard itself governs, and is published by ETSI at <https://www.etsi.org/>.
`sources.md` records this source and its terms.

Clauses 9 (web), 10 (non-web documents) and 11.1 to 11.4 (software)
apply the WCAG criteria, and the WCAG 2.2 table answers them. Clause 8.3,
on the physical access to products that stand in place, is answered as
one row, with its measurements in the remarks. Clauses whose only role is
to group the ones beneath them are not listed.

## 4.2.1 Use without vision

Some mode of operation can be used by a person who cannot see.

## 4.2.2 Use with limited vision

Some mode of operation can be used by a person with limited vision, such as through magnification, contrast or a different layout.

## 4.2.3 Use without perceiving colour

Some visual mode of operation does not require the user to tell colours apart.

## 4.2.4 Use without hearing

Where the product makes sound, some mode of operation can be used by a person who cannot hear.

## 4.2.5 Use with limited hearing

Where the product makes sound, some mode of operation gives better audio for a person with limited hearing.

## 4.2.6 Use without speech, or with limited speech

Where the product takes spoken input, some mode of operation does not require the user to speak.

## 4.2.7 Use with limited manipulation or strength

Some mode of operation can be used without fine motor control, strength or several simultaneous actions.

## 4.2.8 Use with limited reach

The operable parts of a product that stands in place can be reached by everyone it is meant for.

## 4.2.9 Reducing triggers of photosensitive seizures

Where the product shows visual content, some mode of operation keeps flashing low enough not to trigger seizures.

## 4.2.10 Use with limited cognition, language or learning

The product offers features that make it simpler and easier to use for people with limited cognition, language or learning.

## 4.2.11 Privacy of users of accessibility features

A person using an accessibility feature keeps the same privacy as anyone else.

## 5.1.2.1 Closed functionality: general

Where a user cannot attach assistive technology, the product meets the closed-functionality clauses itself.

## 5.1.2.2 Closed functionality: no reliance on assistive technology

Nothing of a closed product requires the user to attach or install assistive technology.

## 5.1.3.1 Non-visual access: general

Where a closed product shows information visually, it also makes that information available without vision.

## 5.1.3.2 Non-visual access: delivering speech

Speech output can be heard through the product's own speaker or a headset, as the user chooses.

## 5.1.3.3 Non-visual access: keeping speech in step

Spoken output corresponds to what is shown and where it is shown.

## 5.1.3.4 Non-visual access: control of speech

Users can pause and repeat the speech output.

## 5.1.3.5 Non-visual access: interrupting speech

Speech output stops when the user acts, unless it is essential.

## 5.1.3.6 Non-visual access: speaking non-text content

Non-text content shown to the user is also described in speech.

## 5.1.3.7 Non-visual access: speaking video information

Information that pre-recorded video shows is also given in speech.

## 5.1.3.8 Non-visual access: masked entry

Masked characters are not spoken unless the user chooses, and are spoken as masked.

## 5.1.3.9 Non-visual access: private personal data

Personal data given in speech is given privately.

## 5.1.3.10 Non-visual access: audio that does not interfere

Other sound the product makes does not drown out its speech output.

## 5.1.3.11 Non-visual access: private listening volume

Private listening has a volume control.

## 5.1.3.12 Non-visual access: speaker volume

Speaker output has a volume control with sufficient range.

## 5.1.3.13 Non-visual access: resetting volume

A product shared by many users resets its volume between users.

## 5.1.3.14 Non-visual access: spoken languages

Speech is in the same language as the content shown.

## 5.1.3.15 Non-visual access: identifying errors

Errors shown to the user are also identified in speech.

## 5.1.3.16 Non-visual access: receipts and tickets

Receipts, tickets and other transactional outputs are also available in speech.

## 5.1.4 Closed functionality: enlarging text

Where a closed product cannot enlarge text, its text is large enough as shown.

## 5.1.5 Closed functionality: seeing auditory information

Information a closed product gives by sound is also given visually.

## 5.1.6.1 Closed functionality: operating without a keyboard

A closed product can be operated without a keyboard or its equivalent.

## 5.1.6.2 Closed functionality: input focus

Where a closed product moves input focus, the focus is never trapped.

## 5.1.7 Closed functionality: access without speech

A closed product that takes speech offers another way to give the same input.

## 5.2 Turning on accessibility features

An accessibility feature can be turned on without the help of that feature.

## 5.3 Biometrics

Where a biological characteristic identifies a user, another way of identifying is offered.

## 5.4 Keeping accessibility information through conversion

Information provided for accessibility survives a conversion of the content.

## 5.5.1 Operable parts: how they are operated

Operable parts do not need tight grasping, pinching or twisting of the wrist, or more than a stated force.

## 5.5.2 Operable parts: telling them apart

Operable parts can be located and identified without vision and without activating them.

## 5.6.1 Locking and toggle controls: status by touch or sound

The status of a locking or toggle control can be told by touch or sound.

## 5.6.2 Locking and toggle controls: status by sight

The status of a locking or toggle control can be told by sight.

## 5.7 Key repeat

Where keys repeat, the delay before repeating and the rate can be adjusted.

## 5.8 Accepting a double strike

Where a key accepts a double strike, the interval in which it does can be adjusted.

## 5.9 Simultaneous user actions

No mode of operation requires the user to act in more than one place at once.

## 6.1 Audio bandwidth for speech

Voice communication carries a sufficiently wide frequency range.

## 6.2.1.1 Real-time text: communicating in text

Where the product offers two-way voice, it also lets users communicate in real-time text.

## 6.2.1.2 Real-time text: voice and text at once

Where voice and real-time text are both offered, they can be used at the same time.

## 6.2.2.1 Real-time text: telling text apart

Text sent and text received are displayed so that they can be told apart.

## 6.2.2.2 Real-time text: direction known to software

Whether a piece of text was sent or received is determinable by software.

## 6.2.2.3 Real-time text: identifying the speaker

Where voice participants are identified, real-time text participants are identified too.

## 6.2.2.4 Real-time text: visual indicator of audio

A visual indicator shows when there is audio activity during a real-time text call.

## 6.2.3 Real-time text: interoperability

Real-time text works with the other systems the clause names.

## 6.2.4 Real-time text: responsiveness

Each character is sent within a short stated delay after it is typed.

## 6.3 Caller identification

Caller identification and similar information is also available in text and by another means than sight.

## 6.4 Alternatives to voice-based services

Services based on voice offer an alternative that does not rely on hearing or speech.

## 6.5.1 Video communication: general

Where the product offers two-way video, the video meets the clauses below for sign language and lip reading.

## 6.5.2 Video communication: resolution

Video is sent at a sufficient resolution.

## 6.5.3 Video communication: frame rate

Video is sent at a sufficient frame rate.

## 6.5.4 Video communication: audio and video in step

Sound and picture stay within a stated time of one another.

## 6.5.5 Video communication: visual indicator of audio

A visual indicator shows when there is audio activity during a video call.

## 6.5.6 Video communication: identifying who signs

Where speakers are identified, a person communicating in sign language is identified too.

## 6.6 Alternatives to video-based services

Services based on video offer an alternative that does not rely on sight or hearing alone.

## 7.1.1 Captions: playing them

Products that play video can show its captions.

## 7.1.2 Captions: keeping them in step

Captions are shown in step with the sound they convey.

## 7.1.3 Captions: keeping them

Products that convert or pass on video keep its captions.

## 7.1.4 Captions: how they look

Users can adjust how captions look.

## 7.1.5 Spoken subtitles

Where video carries subtitles, they can be spoken.

## 7.2.1 Audio description: playing it

Products that play video can play its audio description.

## 7.2.2 Audio description: keeping it in step

Audio description is played in step with the video.

## 7.2.3 Audio description: keeping it

Products that convert or pass on video keep its audio description.

## 7.3 Controls for captions and audio description

Captions and audio description can be turned on at the same level of interaction as the main media controls.

## 8.1.1 Hardware: general

Hardware meets the generic clauses for the functions it has.

## 8.1.2 Hardware: standard connections

Connections for devices users bring follow an industry standard.

## 8.1.3 Hardware: colour

Colour is not the only means of conveying information on the hardware.

## 8.2.1.1 Hardware speech: volume range

Voice output offers a volume range of a stated size.

## 8.2.1.2 Hardware speech: stepping the volume

The volume can be raised in small steps.

## 8.2.2.1 Magnetic coupling: fixed-line devices

Fixed-line devices couple magnetically with hearing technologies.

## 8.2.2.2 Magnetic coupling: wireless devices

Wireless devices couple magnetically with hearing technologies.

## 8.3 Stationary products: physical access

A product that stands in place can be approached and its operable parts reached, as the clause's subclauses measure it.

## 8.4.1 Mechanically operable parts: numeric keys

Numeric keys are laid out conventionally and the 5 key can be found by touch.

## 8.4.2.1 Mechanically operable parts: how they are operated

Mechanical parts can be operated with one hand and without tight grasping, pinching or twisting.

## 8.4.2.2 Mechanically operable parts: force

Mechanical parts can be operated with no more than a stated force.

## 8.4.3 Keys, tickets and fare cards

Keys, tickets and fare cards can be oriented by touch.

## 8.5 Tactile indication of speech mode

A product that offers speech output shows by touch how to start it.

## 11.5.2.1 Accessibility services: software with a user interface

Platform software offers the accessibility services that software with a user interface needs.

## 11.5.2.2 Accessibility services: assistive technologies

Platform software offers the accessibility services that assistive technologies need.

## 11.5.2.3 Accessibility services: using them

Software uses the platform's documented accessibility services.

## 11.5.2.4 Assistive technology: using the services

Assistive technology uses the platform's documented accessibility services.

## 11.5.2.5 Object information

The role, states, boundary, name and description of user interface elements are determinable by software.

## 11.5.2.6 Rows, columns and headers

The row and column of a table cell, and its headers, are determinable by software.

## 11.5.2.7 Values

The current value of an element, and any allowed range or set, are determinable by software.

## 11.5.2.8 Label relationships

Which element labels or is labelled by another is determinable by software.

## 11.5.2.9 Parent and child relationships

The parent and child relationships of elements are determinable by software.

## 11.5.2.10 Text

Text content, its attributes and its boundary on screen are determinable by software.

## 11.5.2.11 Available actions: listing them

The actions an element offers are determinable by software.

## 11.5.2.12 Available actions: carrying them out

The actions an element offers can be carried out by software.

## 11.5.2.13 Focus and selection: tracking them

Focus and selection, and how they change, are determinable by software.

## 11.5.2.14 Focus and selection: changing them

Focus and selection can be changed by software, where the user can change them.

## 11.5.2.15 Change notification

Assistive technology is notified of changes to the information above.

## 11.5.2.16 States and properties: changing them

States and properties the user can change can also be changed by software.

## 11.5.2.17 Values and text: changing them

Values and text the user can change can also be changed by software.

## 11.6.1 Documented accessibility: user control

Software that offers accessibility features lets the user control them.

## 11.6.2 Documented accessibility: no disruption

Software does not disrupt the platform's accessibility features.

## 11.7 User preferences

Software follows the user's platform settings for colour, contrast, font, size and focus.

## 11.8.1 Authoring tools: content technology

Authoring tools support the accessibility features of the formats they produce.

## 11.8.2 Authoring tools: creating accessible content

Authoring tools let authors create content that meets the clauses for web content and documents.

## 11.8.3 Authoring tools: keeping accessibility through transformation

Authoring tools keep accessibility information when they transform content.

## 11.8.4 Authoring tools: help with repairs

Authoring tools that check accessibility suggest how to fix what they find.

## 11.8.5 Authoring tools: templates

Authoring tools that offer templates offer some that produce accessible content.

## 12.1.1 Product documentation: accessibility and compatibility features

Product documentation lists and explains the product's accessibility and compatibility features.

## 12.1.2 Product documentation: accessible documentation

Product documentation is itself available in an accessible form.

## 12.2.2 Support services: information on accessibility features

Support services give information on the product's accessibility and compatibility features.

## 12.2.3 Support services: effective communication

Support services meet the communication needs of people with disabilities.

## 12.2.4 Support services: accessible documentation

Documentation from support services is available in an accessible form.

## 13.1.1 Relay services: communication between users

Relay services let users of different modes of communication reach one another.

## 13.1.2 Relay services: text

Text relay services let text users and voice users talk.

## 13.1.3 Relay services: sign language

Sign relay services let sign language users and voice users talk.

## 13.1.4 Relay services: lip reading

Lip-reading relay services let lip readers and voice users talk.

## 13.1.5 Relay services: captioned telephony

Captioned telephony lets a user hear and read the other party.

## 13.1.6 Relay services: speech to speech

Speech-to-speech relay helps users with speech impairments be understood.

## 13.2 Reaching relay services

Products for two-way communication do not prevent access to relay services.

## 13.3 Reaching emergency services

Products for two-way communication do not prevent access to emergency services through relay.
