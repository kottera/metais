# ADR-009 — W6-6: the limit-calibration suite of D3 item 3, D18 and Amendment A1 item 5

**2026-10-20, PLAN-2026-10-metais-v1 W6-6.** The calibration suite is
`internal/conformance`'s `TestCalibration`, in `calibrate_test.go`, and
its cases are in `calibrate_cases_test.go`. It holds one worst case for
each work and output limit of D3, and A1 item 5's joint worst case twice:
once in the schema files and once in the combined schema. Each case runs
as a process of the built binary. CPU time, user plus system, and peak
resident memory are read from the process state through the runner's
`measured`, so on linux the peak is the process's own. Each case is judged
against D18 item 2's ceilings, and the suite fails naming the case and the
ceiling. No case is refused from metadata, so each is held to 10 s of CPU
and 1 GiB.

At the defaults the suite runs only with `-calibration.record`, by the
command in `docs/dev/calibration.md`. That page also gives the record's
format, `metais.calibration/1`, and what plan §6's act reports back. Every
`go test` runs `TestCalibrationLowered` instead. It runs each case scaled
down, under limits lowered in the ratio the case has at the default, and
proves each one reaches its limit. A refused case is refused by its limit.
An accepted case is accepted at its limit and refused at one less.
`TestCalibrationJointLocations` proves that the joint cases' locations
sit at `schema-location-length`. No default is changed.

`schema-work` is not counted by the tool (W2-10, ADR-010's log). Its case
does one evaluation more than the default allows and is measured, and its
record says `reached: false`. `TestCalibrationLowered` pins the case short
of its limit, so the change that counts `schema-work` turns that test red.

The dispatch sandbox ran the suite at the defaults: linux/amd64,
go1.26.9, 12 CPUs, on a host shared with other Work Orders. Plan §5 makes
these numbers, not evidence. They are recorded so that §6's act knows where
to look, and R1-015 escalates any breach that act confirms.
