# ADR-009 — W6-10: the hostile corpus of D17 item 6, schemas and patterns, and the schema limits at their bounds

**2026-10-20, PLAN-2026-10-metais-v1 W6-10.** Every case D17 item 6 names
is a `security` vector, or an earlier vector stands for it. Each package
is a source directory with one defect. The files too long or too regular
to write by hand are written by `internal/vectorgen/schemas_test.go`, and
`TestSchemaCorpus` reproduces them. A case whose hostility is in the
package's schemas refuses at its `open` step, through every command the
tool declares as opening a package, under `bounds` of 500 ms of CPU and
64 MiB. A case whose hostility is in the inputs refuses at its `validate`
step. Each is refused by its own limit or rule: no package comes near
`schema-subschemas` or `schema-location-length` at their defaults.

- **References.** `sec/schema-ref-file` (`file:///etc/passwd`),
  `sec/schema-ref-decoy` (a schema only the working directory holds, which
  the case's setup puts there), `dat/schema-ref-remote`
  (`https://example.com/s.json`), `dat/schema-ref-outside`
  (`../outside.json`) and `sec/schema-remote-dialect` (a remote `$schema`).
  `dat/schema-ref-escapes` stands for a reference climbing above the root.
- **Chains, cycles and work.** `sec/ref-depth` (a chain of 32 references
  at the default, of 32 past a lowered 31, and of 33 past the default),
  `sec/ref-cycle` (a cycle that consumes no instance) and
  `sec/schema-work` (four levels of `anyOf` over references at a lowered
  bound of exactly their 65 units and one past it, and thirty levels,
  2^30 paths, refused before they run).
- **Sizes and depth.** `sec/schema-depth`, `sec/schema-size` and
  `sec/combined-schema-size`, each at a lowered bound and one past it.
  `schema-depth` is shown lowered because `value-depth` refuses a schema
  file first at the defaults: a subschema at depth 65 is a value nested at
  least 66 deep.
- **Patterns.** `sec/pattern-backtracking` (`^(a+)+$` against a 65,536-byte
  run of `a` at a lowered `input-size`), `sec/pattern-lookahead`,
  `sec/pattern-lookbehind`, `sec/pattern-backreference` and
  `sec/pattern-named-backreference` (refused),
  `sec/pattern-whitespace`, `sec/pattern-dot` and `sec/pattern-empty-class`
  (translated, each validating the code points where ECMA-262 and RE2
  disagree), `sec/pattern-repeat-bound` (`x{1000}` accepted, `x{1001}`
  refused), `sec/pattern-length` and `sec/regex-program` (each at the
  default and one past it), and `dat/schema-format-regex`.
- **Values.** `dat/unique-items-million` (a million items whose one
  duplicate is the last) and `dat/multiple-of-extremes` (`1e308` against
  `multipleOf` `1e-308`, a multiple, and `3e-308`, not).

Chapter 14 states the profile in a section of its own, as MTS-SEC-040 to
MTS-SEC-042, with `schema-size`, `combined-schema-size`, `schema-depth`,
`ref-depth` and `schema-work` as MTS-SEC-043 to MTS-SEC-046. Chapter 5
states `uniqueItems` and `multipleOf` as MTS-DAT-013 and MTS-DAT-014.

The corpus found three defects, each fixed red first:

- **`ref-depth` was enforced nowhere.** A chain past it was accepted,
  and a cycle reached the library's own cycle error at validation as
  `internal.error`. It is now a pre-check over each compilation's schema
  files. A `$dynamicRef` whose target declares its anchor is taken to
  reach every subschema declaring it, which is the stricter reading.
- **`schema-work` was counted nowhere**, so nested `anyOf` ran without
  bound. It is now counted before the library evaluates anything, by
  METAIS's own walk of the compiled schema and the instance, whatever any
  evaluation's outcome, and charged to the command's meter beside
  `regex-work`. A unit is a subschema applied, and one more per member or
  item there, because the library visits every member of an object at
  every evaluation.
- **A schema file at exactly `schema-depth` gave `internal.error`** at
  validation: the combined schema embeds each file a level below its root,
  and its pre-check measured that level against the files' bound.

**For calibration (R1-015).** On darwin/arm64, in a shared sandbox, the
library spends about 50 µs of CPU on each evaluation of the nested-`anyOf`
shape: 16 levels, about 200,000 units, took 10 s, and 18 levels took
41 s. At that rate a validation within the default `schema-work` of
100,000,000 units runs for more than an hour, far past D18's ceilings.
This item changes no default. W6-6's suite and §6's act measure it on real
hosts, and a lower default is an amendment for Ed.
