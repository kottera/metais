# ADR-011 — W7-E1: the §4.1 conformance report, and SC1's runbook

**2026-10-20, PLAN-2026-10-metais-v1 W7-E1.** The §4.1 example package is
under `examples/accessibility-conformance-report/`, and Wave 6's harness,
`TestExamplePackages`, runs over it: check and test green, packed and
green again, and previewed with `--optional all` and `none` into output
that validates (SC4).

- **The inputs.** `report`, in YAML or JSON, holds the product, the
  vendor, the date, the evaluation methods as prose, optional notes and
  the edition: `wcag`, `section508`, `en301549` or `international`. Each
  standard's table is a CSV input of its own, `wcag`, `section508` and
  `en301549`, whose rows are a criterion number from the standard's
  catalogue, a conformance level from the PRD's five, and remarks.
- **The edition decides the tables** through `input_constraints`,
  `inputs/edition.schema.json`: each edition requires its own tables and
  refuses every other, so validation reports a table missing as
  `input.missing` and a table too many as `input.forbidden`, about the
  table. The edition defaults to `wcag`, and its schema's one example is
  `international`. The generator ignores the input constraints and omits
  every optional input under `--optional none`, so the default is what
  makes the `none` preview valid (the WCAG edition, with only the WCAG
  table), and the example what makes the `all` preview valid (the
  international edition, with all three).
- **The outputs.** `acr.docx` is an `ooxml` container whose six parts are
  templates of their XML, the document and the core properties escaped as
  `html`. Check reports no `template.ooxml.*` finding, so no construct
  splits a text run. `acr.json` is the same report for programs, rendered
  with the `json` filter and validated against
  `references/acr.schema.json` on every render.
- **The references** hold each table's criteria, one section per
  criterion, and `sources.md` records each source. Every criterion's
  summary, and every EN 301 549 clause name, is written in this project's
  own words; no text of ITI's VPAT is used. The catalogue is written three
  times: each table's schema's `enum`, its label partial under
  `templates/criteria/`, and its reference file. `TestACRCatalogueInStep`
  holds the three to one list in one order, with one name and, for WCAG,
  one level for each criterion. It was red with a reference heading
  renamed, and with a number taken out of the WCAG level partial.

`TestACREditionDecidesTables`, in `internal/cli/example_acr_test.go`, runs
`validate` over every edition, and no edition, with every subset of the
tables. It holds the findings to exactly the tables missing and the
tables too many. It was red, in 31 of its 40 cases, with
`input_constraints` removed from the manifest. `TestACRRowsRefused` holds
the tables to their catalogue, their five levels, their required columns
and the control characters a Word document cannot carry. It was red with
the prose pattern widened to `.*`.

`docs/dev/rounds/sc1.md` is the runbook for SC1's `dogfood-round`: what
the round establishes, what the harness is given (a binary of a named
commit, the package packed, and a real product), what it must not read,
and the evidence to report back.

**The terms check's verdicts** (W7-A1, ruled 2026-10-20) are recorded in
the package's `references/sources.md`, each source with its terms, the
notice they require and the verdict. WCAG 2.2's numbers, names and levels
are copied as excerpts, with W3C's notice, link and status in
`references/wcag-2.2.md`. The Revised Section 508 Standards are a United
States Government work. The EN 301 549 table reports against V4.1.1
(2026-09), as Ed ruled: its catalogue was rebuilt against that edition's
clause list, and every clause name and summary is the project's own,
none ETSI's heading. OpenACR is recorded as public domain, with nothing
taken from it. `TestACREditions` holds every file that names EN 301
549's edition to V4.1.1, and the WCAG reference to W3C's notice. It was
red with the document template naming V3.2.1, and with the notice's
"W3C®" changed.
