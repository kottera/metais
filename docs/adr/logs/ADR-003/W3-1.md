# ADR-003 — W3-1: the ACR refuses a criterion answered twice or a required criterion missing

**2026-10-23, PLAN-2026-10-metais-values-and-rules W3-1.** The
accessibility conformance report example adopts Amendment A2's two
keywords, items 11 to 13. Each of its three criteria tables' arrays
carries `uniqueKeys: ["/criterion"]` and a `coverage` keyed on
`/criterion`, beside `minItems` and `maxItems`. The change adds no
specification text and no finding code. On `main` at `19d7f9d0`, the
external tester's table of plan §1 validated with no finding: 1.1.1
answered `Supports` and then `Does Not Support`, and no other row. Now
one `validate` run refuses it with one `input.unique_keys` at
`/1/criterion` and 54 `input.coverage` findings at the table, whose
`missing` values are the other Level A and AA criteria.
`TestACRTesterTableRefused` holds that, and
`TestACRCriterionAnsweredOnce` holds the repeat rule in all three
tables.

**WCAG 2.2's `coverage`** is the 55 criteria that
`templates/criteria/wcag-level.liquid` names Level A or AA, in the
standard's order, and no Level AAA criterion (plan §8).
`TestACRTablesCovered` holds the list equal to the partial's, so a Level
A or AA criterion added to or dropped from one without the other fails.
Level AAA rows stay optional.

**The Revised Section 508 and EN 301 549 tables** cover every criterion
their `enum` takes: chapters 3 to 6 for Section 508 (104), and every
EN 301 549 V4.1.1 clause outside clauses 9, 10 and 11.1 to 11.4 (148).
This is a schema decision, not a new judgement about what the report
means, for three reasons:

- The package had already made that judgement. `inputs/tables.md`
  says a report lists every criterion of the table, "so that a reader can
  tell a criterion met from one left out". It says a criterion that does
  not concern the product is `Not Applicable`, and that "for software,
  chapter 4's hardware criteria are Not Applicable". So a chapter the
  product has no part in is answered row by row, not left out. Coverage
  turns that stated requirement into a refusal.
- Neither standard has levels, so nothing marks a subset as the
  required one, as Level A and AA does in WCAG. Any subset would be the
  package inventing a scope the standard does not draw.
- R-W1-c already gives every product an honest answer to every row. A
  criterion that does not concern the product is `Not Applicable`. One
  that applies but cannot be assessed, such as a third-party evaluator's
  602 and 603 or clause 12, is `Not Evaluated` with remarks saying why.
  So full coverage never forces a false answer.

`inputs/tables.md` and the guide in `metais.yaml` now say that a
criterion answered twice, and a criterion a table must answer but leaves
out, are refused.

**The shipped tables.** `international` already answered every
criterion of all three tables, so its inputs and expected tree are
unchanged. `wcag_only` grows from seven rows to the 55 Level A and AA
criteria, plus its one Level AAA row (2.4.13, `Not Evaluated` with
remarks), in WCAG's order. Its expected tree, regenerated by
`metais test --update`, changes only in its rows and its summary counts:
56 criteria, 41 `Supports`, 1 `Partially Supports`, 1 `Does Not
Support`, 12 `Not Applicable` and 1 `Not Evaluated`. Its level claim stays
"Levels A, AA and AAA", because its rows already spanned all three
levels.

**The earlier rules' tests.** `TestACRRequiredLevelsEvaluated` already
answers all 86 criteria and is unchanged. `TestACRNotEvaluatedSaysWhy`
and `TestACRRowsRefused` now put their rows into the examples' complete
tables, through `acrTable`, and compute the rows' indexes. Each still
asserts exactly its rule's findings and no `input.coverage`.
`TestACRClaimsFromRows` needs tables that leave a level or a chapter out,
which coverage now refuses. So it renders a copy of the package with
`coverage` removed (`acrUncovered`), where its cases still prove that
the claims follow the rows. `TestACRSampleRendered` holds
`render --sample` with `--items` 1, 3 and 54, below WCAG's 55 covered
criteria. Each renders with no finding, answers every covered criterion
once, and writes the same bytes for one seed.
